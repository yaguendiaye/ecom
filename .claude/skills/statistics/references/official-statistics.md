# Official statistics, regional harmonisation and trade in services

## 1. International frameworks to cite

| Domain | Standard |
|---|---|
| National accounts | SNA 2008; SNA 2025 (being implemented) |
| Balance of payments | IMF BPM6; BPM7 (adopted 2025, gradual implementation) |
| Trade in services | MSITS 2010 (Manual on Statistics of International Trade in Services) and its Compiler's Guide; update in line with BPM7 in progress |
| Services classification | EBOPS 2010 (extended balance of payments services); CPC Ver.2.1 |
| Activity classification | ISIC Rev.4 (Rev.5 adopted 2023) |
| Foreign affiliates | FATS (inward/outward) |
| Merchandise trade | IMTS 2010, HS nomenclature |
| Quality | UN National Quality Assurance Frameworks (UN NQAF); IMF DQAF; Fundamental Principles of Official Statistics |
| Data exchange | SDMX |
| Regional | ECOWAS regional statistical programme and strategy; AU Strategy for the Harmonisation of Statistics in Africa (SHaSA 2); UEMOA/AFRISTAT methodologies |

Always verify current versions and dates before citing — standards are being
revised (BPM7, SNA 2025, MSITS update). Do not invent document numbers.

## 2. Trade in services: key concepts

**Four modes of supply (GATS):**
- Mode 1 — Cross-border supply (e.g. remote consulting, software delivered online).
- Mode 2 — Consumption abroad (e.g. tourism, students abroad, medical treatment).
- Mode 3 — Commercial presence (foreign affiliate; measured via FATS).
- Mode 4 — Presence of natural persons (e.g. an engineer temporarily abroad).

**Main data sources:** ITRS (international transactions reporting systems via
banks), enterprise surveys of services trade, administrative data (tourism
arrivals, transport), mirror statistics from partner countries, FATS surveys,
digital platform data.

**Common compilation issues in Member States:**
- Under-coverage of informal and small exporters; reliance on ITRS thresholds.
- Mobile-money and fintech flows not captured.
- Mode allocation (simplified approach: assign EBOPS items to dominant mode).
- Partner-country breakdown missing → bilateral asymmetries.
- Travel estimates from outdated border surveys.
- Digitally delivered trade and e-commerce (OECD-WTO-IMF-UNCTAD Handbook on
  Measuring Digital Trade).

**Analytical indicators:** services trade balance, export share of GDP,
revealed comparative advantage (RCA = (X_ij/X_i) / (X_wj/X_w)), export
concentration (HHI), intra-regional share, growth decomposition, mirror
asymmetry ratio = (exports_A→B − imports_B←A) / average.

## 3. Regional harmonisation (ECOWAS Member States)

When comparing countries:
- Same concept, definition, classification and reference period.
- Same currency basis (current USD, constant prices, or PPP — state which;
  WAEMU members use FCFA pegged to EUR).
- Document metadata per country (SDMX/SIMS structure): source, coverage,
  methodology, revisions, release calendar.
- Flag breaks in series (rebasing of national accounts is frequent).
- Prefer medians and ranges to regional averages dominated by one large economy;
  give both weighted and unweighted aggregates.

## 4. Academic report structure

1. Executive summary (key findings with figures).
2. Context and objectives.
3. Methodology (sources, concepts, sample design, estimation, quality controls).
4. Results (tables with n, CI/CV, footnotes; charts).
5. Limitations and data quality assessment.
6. Recommendations (for compilers, for policy).
7. Annexes: questionnaires, metadata, tables, technical notes.
References in a consistent style (APA or the client's style).

## 5. Capacity-building support

When supporting national compilers: start from their existing sources, propose
a phased roadmap (quick wins → full compliance), provide templates
(questionnaires, compilation spreadsheets, metadata sheets), worked examples
with their own data, and a checklist of quality checks before dissemination.
